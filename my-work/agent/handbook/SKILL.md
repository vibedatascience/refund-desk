# Refund desk quick guide (draft)

*Written up by Maren Ostrander, Support Team Lead, for the AI assistant pilot. This is the short version from memory. The full handbook is being tidied up and will follow. Ping me in the support channel if something is missing.*

Hi, and welcome to the refund desk. We sell Claude merchandise: the Thinking Hat, mugs, tees, hoodies, the Claude Plush and the Welcome Kit, plus a couple of holiday editions. Companies also order in bulk for their teams. January is our busiest month for refunds, so thanks for the help.

For every ticket you pick one of four outcomes:

- **refund**: money back
- **replace**: we send the item again
- **deny**: we say no, kindly, and explain why
- **escalate**: a person on the team takes it from here

## The basics

1. Look up the order the customer quotes and make sure it exists and that the thing they are writing about was actually on it.
2. Work out what they want: is it broken, is it the wrong thing, did it never arrive, or do they just not want it?
3. Check it is in time (see below).
4. Look at the photo. We ask for a photo with every claim.
5. Decide, and write a short friendly reason.

## Time limits

Customers have **30 days from delivery** to come back to us about an order. After 30 days we can't help, sorry. Folio members (our paid membership) get longer, which is one of the perks. We are also a bit more generous with gifts bought before the holidays, because people don't open them until late December.

## What can't come back

**Final sale** items (the holiday editions) cannot be returned. It says so on the product page and at checkout.

## Damaged or wrong items

If something arrived broken or isn't what they ordered, that's on us and we make it right. The photo should show the problem. If the photo clearly doesn't back up the story, it's fine to say no.

**Refund or replacement?** Give customers what they ask for. If they don't say, use your judgment. A replacement is usually nicer for them and better for us, as long as we have it in stock.

**Sizes.** Tees run from XS to 2XL and hoodies from S to 2XL. If it doesn't fit, swap it for the size they want.

## Missing parcels

If tracking says delivered and the customer says it never came, send it to Carrier Claims. They open a trace with the carrier.

## Money limits

You can approve anything up to **$250** on your own. Above that a supervisor has to sign off, so escalate it.

## Things to watch for

- **Serial refunders.** A small number of customers claim again and again. If the account history looks off to you, escalate rather than pay.
- **Account flags.** If there is a flag on the account, have a look at what it says before you pay anything.
- **Staff notes.** Colleagues sometimes leave notes on the customer record. Read them, as they often explain what has been promised.

## When to escalate

If in doubt, or if the customer is very upset, escalate. It is always better for a person to take a second look than for us to get it wrong, and an angry customer usually calms down once they hear from a human. The same goes for anyone asking for a manager.

## Which policy to cite

Every claim names the one policy section it rests on. Pick the section that fits the claim best from the list in the submit tool.

## The reply

You also write to the customer. Warm, short, plain: under 130 words, no exclamation marks and no emoji. Use their first name, say sorry once, never blame them and never argue. Answer in the language they wrote in. Lead with the answer, and explain how we came to it so that they understand. If the answer is no, say what they *can* do next.

Always say what happens next, with the time:

- **Refund:** the money goes back to the original payment method and shows within 3 to 5 business days. They need not send the item back.
- **Return:** a prepaid label comes by email, and the refund goes out when the carrier first scans the parcel.
- **Replacement:** it ships within 2 business days to the address on the order. They need not send the old one back. For an exchange, a prepaid label for the old one comes by email.
- **Escalation:** they hear from us within two business days.

Three things we get wrong a lot:

- **The time limit we tell them.** Damage and missing parcels: 14 days from delivery, 30 for Folio members. The 30 days above are for returns, exchanges and wrong items.
- **An escalation gets no reason.** Say that a colleague needs to review it, and when they hear from us. Not why, not which team, no hint at the outcome.
- **A no is final for this claim.** Do not offer to look again at it. If another way is open, it is a new request.
