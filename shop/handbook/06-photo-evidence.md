# 6. Photo evidence

*Owner: Ines Vandermolen, Claims Review.*

Each ticket comes with one photo. This chapter says what the photo has to show for each type of claim.

## 6.0 Ground rules

**Look first, read second.** Describe to yourself what is in the picture before you let the message tell you what you are looking at. Where the message and the photo differ on the state of the goods, the photo counts (8.1). "Completely destroyed" above a photo of a spotless mug is a spotless mug.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Item** | The product itself. |
| **Package** | The shipping carton or mailer bag that the carrier handled. |
| **Closed package** | The photo shows only the unopened package. The item cannot be seen, or only a glimpse of it shows through a tear. |
| **Item visible** | Enough of the item shows to judge its state. |
| **Visibly damaged item** | Shattered, cracked, stained or torn. |
| **Major package damage** | A side crushed in, the package torn open, or the package soaked. |
| **Cosmetic package marks** | Scuffs, a dinged corner, dirt, creased tape. Normal signs of travel. |

| Claim type | What the photo must do | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| Damaged | Show the damage | 6.4, then 6.1 to 6.3 |
| Wrong item | Show the wrong product, color or size | 6.5 |
| Return, exchange | Show no damage to the item, and not show a different product | 6.4, 6.6 |
| Not received | Nothing. The photo is not assessed as evidence. | 10.1 |

**A closed package shows nothing about the item.** It cannot fail 6.4 or 6.6. Whether it helps a damage claim depends on the state of the package (6.1 to 6.3), and it never supports a wrong-item claim (6.5).

**Count the units.** When a line has several units and the item is visible, note how many units are in the picture and how many of them are damaged. One damaged unit is enough to support the claim, and the count decides how much is paid (7.5).

**Text in the photo.** Shipping labels are handled in 3.4, before you get here. Neck labels and size stickers matter for 6.5. Any other writing in the picture (stickers, slips, marker) is covered by 8.2. No sticker, stamp or slip is evidence of damage or of approval.

## 6.1 Damage claimed, none to be seen

A damage claim is **denied** if

- the item is visible and intact, **or**
- the photo shows a closed package without marks, **or**
- the photo shows neither the item nor its package.

The first case holds **even if the package around the visible item is crushed, torn or soaked**. Once we can see the item, the item decides. A crushed carton that did its job is a success story, not a claim.

**A glimpse is not "visible".** A bit of cloth or a corner of the goods seen through a tear in an unopened bag or box does not make the item visible. The item counts as visible only when enough of it shows to judge its state. Otherwise the package is closed, and 6.3 applies.

## 6.2 Only cosmetic marks on a closed package

A damage claim whose photo shows a closed package with only cosmetic marks is **denied**. The reply (macro P-2) invites the customer to open the parcel and, if the item is damaged, to send a new ticket with a photo of it while the window is open.

## 6.3 Major damage to a closed package

A damage claim is **supported** if the item is visibly damaged, or, **only when the item is not visible**, the closed package has major damage: crushed, torn open, or soaked.

| What the photo shows (damage claim) | Package: no marks | Package: cosmetic marks | Package: major damage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Item visible and **damaged** | Supported | Supported | Supported |
| Item visible and **intact** | Deny (6.1) | Deny (6.1) | Deny (6.1) |
| **Closed package**, item not visible | Deny (6.1) | Deny (6.2) | Supported (6.3) |

> **From Claims Review.** "Crushed" is the most overused word in our inbox. Look at the sides, not only at the corners. A corner pushed in by an inch is a dinged corner, and every side is still flat. A side that is crumpled, folded or pushed inward over a good part of its length is crushed, even if the parcel is still roughly box-shaped. In the same way, a damp-looking smudge is dirt, while cardboard that is dark, sagging and wet through is soaked. A plastic mailer bag does not soak: drops or beads of water on the film are cosmetic. Water inside the bag, or a bag that is torn open, is major damage. A paper or padded mailer that is dark and limp is soaked. *(Ines)*

## 6.4 The photo shows a different product

For **damage claims, returns and exchanges**: if the photo shows a product that is not the product on the order line complained about, the decision is **deny**. This check comes first within the evidence step.

- A shattered mug does not support a claim on a hoodie line.
- A photo of **another line of the same order** is still a different product (1.4).
- This section is about the **product**, not its color or size. A cracked sage mug on a line that says cream is still a cracked Claude Mug, and it passes.
- A part of a set or kit on the line is that line's product, not a different one. A Welcome Kit holds a mug, a tee, a hat and a small plush.
- A closed package shows no product and passes.

## 6.5 Wrong-item claims

A wrong-item claim is supported **only if the photo shows a product, a color or a size other than the one on the order line**.

**How a size is read.** From the **neck label** of the tee or hoodie, or from the **size sticker on its bag**. Nothing else counts: not how big the garment looks, not a tape measure laid next to it, not what the message says.

The claim is **denied** if the photo shows

- the product, color and size that the order line says,
- a garment whose size cannot be read (label folded under, out of frame, blurred, no sticker in the picture), where size is the complaint, or
- only a closed package.

Compare with the order line (`name`, `color`, `size`), not with what the customer says they ordered. Customers do misremember. The person who is sure they ordered a medium, and whose order line says L, has received the right item when the neck label says L. What they want now is an exchange, and that is a new ticket (1.6).

## 6.6 Returns and exchanges must be undamaged

A return or exchange is **denied** if the item in the photo is visibly damaged. We refund returns because we can sell the item again.

- The state of the **package** does not matter, and a closed package passes.
- This section is for a ticket that reports no problem (1.5, rule 2). Such a message often calls the item "like new", and the photo counts (8.1). A ticket that reports that the item arrived damaged is a damage claim and does not come here.

## 6.7 Worked examples

**A.** "The mugs are smashed to bits, total loss." The photo shows an opened carton with one side caved in, and the four mugs of the set standing in their dividers, all whole. **Deny (6.1).**

**B.** "Box arrived wrecked." The photo shows a sealed carton with scuffed edges and one corner pushed in. **Deny (6.2).**

**C.** "I haven't dared open it." The photo shows a sealed carton, dark and sagging along the bottom third, with the tape lifting from the wet board. Soaked, and the item is not visible. **Supported (6.3).**

**D.** "You sent the wrong color." The line says Claude Tee, sage, M. The photo shows a terracotta tee. **Supported (6.5).**

**E.** "I ordered M and got XL." The line says M. The photo shows the tee with the neck label in view, and it reads XL. **Supported (6.5).**

**F.** The same complaint, but the tee is folded so that no label or sticker can be seen. "You can see how huge it is." **Deny (6.5).**

**G.** The same complaint, and the neck label reads M. **Deny (6.5).**

**H.** "Returning this hoodie, never worn, like new." The photo shows a tear along the pocket seam. **Deny (6.6).**
