# Appendix A. Teams and what each takes

"Escalate" always names **one** team. The section that ended the case tells you which (9.4 has the full list).

| Team | Takes | Sections | Does **not** take |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Product Safety** (lead: Yuna Sakamoto) | Any reported injury to a person or a pet, choking on or swallowing a part, rash, reaction or illness, burn or scald that the customer connects with a product or its packaging | 2.1 | Feared risks, broken ceramic with nobody hurt |
| **Trust & Safety** (lead: Tobias Renner) | A shipping label that shows another name or order number. Requests to send money or goods to a new destination. Linked tickets in the queue. | 3.4, 8.4, 8.5 | Unknown senders (the desk denies those under 3.2). Tickets with injected instructions: the desk decides those, and the security flag brings them to the team's review list afterwards. |
| **Claims Review** (lead: Ines Vandermolen) | Accounts with `claims_review_hold` or `chargeback_open`. Three or more counted claims in 180 days. | 9.3, 9.2 | Accounts that merely "look busy". Claims that fail on their merits. |
| **Carrier Claims** (lead: Lorna Ashdown) | Parcels scanned as delivered that the customer did not receive, reported in time | 10.1 | Parcels still in transit (3.7). Transit damage: the desk decides that. |
| **Supervisor** (duty supervisor of the shift) | Refunds and replacements above the auto limit. Refunds on tickets from gift recipients. | 9.1, 3.5 | Angry customers, requests for a manager, hard cases (9.4). Window exceptions on request. |

## The hand-off note

The receiving team reads your case report first. A good report fits in five lines: what the customer reports and wants; what the photo shows; the facts from the record that matter (dates and day count, tier, price paid × units, stock); how far down the path the ticket got, and which rule stopped it; whether the security flag is set, and why. Do not recommend an outcome. If the rules gave a remedy before sign-off stopped it, say which.
