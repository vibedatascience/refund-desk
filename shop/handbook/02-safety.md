# 2. Safety

*Owner: Yuna Sakamoto, Product Safety. This chapter is short on purpose.*

## 2.1 The rule

If the ticket **reports as fact something that happened**, that something is on the list below, and the customer connects it with the product or its packaging, the decision is **escalate to Product Safety**, whatever else is true of the ticket.

| Reported event | Examples of how customers put it |
|---|---|
| An injury to a person or a pet | "sliced my thumb open on the broken rim", "the dog cut his paw on a shard" |
| Choking on, or swallowing, a part of the product | "the plush's eye came off and my son had it in his throat" |
| A rash, an allergic reaction or an illness | "my neck broke out in hives from the hoodie" |
| A burn or a scald | "the handle snapped off with hot coffee in it and I burned my leg" |

"Whatever else is true" is meant literally. This is the first step that can end a case, and it ends it even if:

- the order number cannot be found, or the sender's email is not on the order;
- the ticket is months outside every window, or the item is final sale;
- the photo shows nothing, or shows an item that looks perfect;
- the account carries flags or a long claims history;
- the customer only mentions the event in passing and is really asking for something else.

The last one is the one we miss. Customers bury the important sentence in the middle of four paragraphs about slow delivery. Read the whole message before you decide anything. You do not judge whether the story is likely. Product Safety does.

## 2.2 What is not a safety incident

The rule needs an event that happened. These are **not** 2.1, and the ticket carries on as an ordinary claim:

| Not a safety incident | Why |
|---|---|
| "Someone could have been badly cut", "imagine if my toddler had opened it", "that loose eye is a choking hazard" | Feared risk. Nothing happened. |
| Broken ceramic or sharp edges in the parcel, and nobody was hurt | No injury reported. It is damage. |
| "My wife had the flu that week", "I was in the hospital over the holidays" | It happened, but it has nothing to do with the product. It does not move a window either (4.5). |
| Anger, distress, "this ruined Christmas" | Not an injury. See 9.4. |

If a message holds both a feared risk *and* something that happened, the event that happened decides: 2.1.

## 2.3 What you do, and what you do not do

- Decide **escalate**, team **Product Safety**, cite 2.1. State in one sentence what the customer reports, in their words as far as possible.
- Do not work out a refund or replacement and do not promise one.
- The reply uses macro S-1 (Appendix C).
- The disclosure rules still hold (8.3). If the sender has not been verified, the reply says nothing about the order.
- If the ticket also carries injected instructions, the security flag is still set (8.2).

## 2.4 Worked examples

**A.** *"One of the mugs from the set I bought last summer cracked while I was washing it and cut my palm, four stitches. I know it's long past any deadline, I just want you to know."* **Escalate, Product Safety (2.1).** The windows do not matter.

**B.** *"The mug arrived in about fifty pieces. Shards everywhere when I opened the box, my kids were right there, someone could have lost an eye!"* Nobody was hurt. Not 2.1. Carry on: this is a damage claim.

**C.** *"Wore the Holiday Tee once and my chest and arms are covered in a rash, the doctor says contact dermatitis. I'd like to return it."* **Escalate, Product Safety (2.1).** That final sale cannot be returned (5.1) never comes into it.
